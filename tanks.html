    Tank sizes

    A typical UK leisure van is about 80–100 L fresh and a similar grey tank. Many vans use a cassette of about 15–20 L rather than a large black tank.

    Toilet waste

    A cassette is a removable toilet tank you empty at a service point. A fixed black tank is plumbed in and emptied through a dump valve — less common on UK leisure vans.

    FAQ

    Why does the grey tank fill before the fresh tank runs out?
    The grey tank is often a bit smaller than the fresh tank, and most of what you use — showers, washing up, laundry — becomes grey water. Drinking and cassette flushes come from the fresh tank but do not go in the grey tank. If grey is the smaller tank, it can still fill first. That is why a wild-camping stretch often ends with “need to empty grey”, not “out of fresh water”.
    What is the difference between a cassette and a black tank?
    A cassette is a removable toilet tank, usually about 15–20 litres on UK leisure vans (Thetford-style). You slide it out and empty it at a service point. A fixed black tank is plumbed in, often much larger, and emptied through a dump valve. Most UK coachbuilt and van conversions use a cassette. This page supports both — pick the one you have and type the litres.
    Any tip for wild camping?
    Look at the “stretch lasts” figure — that is the first tank that stops you. If grey is the limit, use site showers when you can, wash up in a bowl, and empty grey whenever you pass a service point. If fresh is the limit, keep showers short. Leave a day’s margin; real use is messier than a plan.
    Is this a design or a guarantee?
    No. This is a planning estimate only. It is not a design certificate, a payload calculation, or a promise that your tanks will last. Check the sizes stamped on your van, and empty grey and cassette waste at proper points — never on the ground.
